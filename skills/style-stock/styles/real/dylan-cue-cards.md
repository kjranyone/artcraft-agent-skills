# REAL STOCK: Bob Dylan "Subterranean Homesick Blues" (1965) — the cue-card grammar

**Work**: "Subterranean Homesick Blues" opening sequence of D.A. Pennebaker's
documentary *Dont Look Back* (1967), filmed May 1965 in an alley behind the
Savoy Hotel, London. Widely called the first modern music video. Cue cards
written by Dylan (+ Allen Ginsberg, Bob Neuwirth in the background).
**Provenance**: ✅ decomposed from well-documented sources (TIME oral
history; Telegraph "accidentally invented the pop video"; Music Video Wiki).

**Why this work matters**: THE origin of the lyric motion clip. One long
take, one alley, Dylan flipping large hand-written cue cards and DROPPING
each as its lyric passes. Its lesson is brutal and still unmatched: **the
lyric card must drop exactly on the word, and nothing else may exist.**

## Decomposition

**Structure**: one continuous take (no cuts). Cards flip/drop on their lyric,
one card per line. Intentional misspellings and puns on the cards. The
background (alley, people) is ignored by the performer — staging is total.

**Tokens**:
- cards: large white boards, hand-written marker text, held to fill ~60% of
  frame height
- the performer: static body, only hands move
- no effects, no grade, no graphics — the DROP is the only event

**The grammar**:
1. One card = one lyric line. Never two lines per card.
2. The drop/flip happens ON the syllable. Early or late is a mistake.
3. Cards are thrown/dropped to the ground — the discard is part of the
   rhythm (visual percussion).
4. Imperfection (hand writing, misspellings) is charm, not error.
5. One take. If the take is good, it ships.

## EffectCraft recipe (video translation)

- Full-frame "card" solids or big text layers; each card = one line.
- Card swap EXACTLY on the lyric beat (hold-keyframe pop, 1 frame).
- Add 2-4° random rotation per card + slight x jitter (hand-held feel).
- Micro shake: 2-3 px position noise keys every 6 frames.
- Optional: drop-out animation — card falls 40 px with fade in 3 frames
  as the next appears.

## Anti-patterns

- Multiple lines on one card; cards visible longer than their lyric.
- Smooth easing between cards (cards CUT, they don't glide).
- Polished graphics on the cards (hand-written or diegetic look only).

## Best for

lyric videos, beat-synced messaging, "one take" credibility segments.
GRAND2: the copy cards + BGM beat grammar transfer directly.
